# Human–AI Co-Agency  
*A book in the CloudPedagogy AI Capability Series*

**Status:** Final (v1.0)

---

## Overview

**Human–AI Co-Agency** is an open-access book published by **CloudPedagogy** as part of the **AI Capability Series**. The book examines one of the most critical and often under-specified dimensions of AI adoption: **how agency, responsibility, and judgement are distributed when humans work with AI systems**.

Rather than treating AI as a neutral tool or an autonomous actor, this book frames AI-supported work as a **co-agential arrangement**—one in which human authority must be intentionally preserved, exercised, and renewed. It addresses the practical question that underpins ethical, effective, and trustworthy AI use:

> *Who decides, who is responsible, and who remains accountable when AI is involved?*

The book is written for educators, researchers, professionals, leaders, and institutions seeking to use AI without eroding human agency, professional judgement, or institutional trust.

---

## Purpose of the Book

This book is designed to:

- Clarify what **human–AI co-agency** means in practice  
- Explain why agency is the **central capability challenge** in AI-supported work  
- Identify common risks such as automation bias, responsibility drift, and over-trust  
- Provide a practical framework for **designing, stabilising, and renewing agency** over time  

The book does not argue against AI use. Instead, it shows how AI can be integrated into human systems **without surrendering decision authority or accountability**.

## Position in the AI Capability Series

Within the **CloudPedagogy AI Capability Framework**, Human–AI Co-Agency is treated as a **core capability domain**. It underpins the functioning of all other domains:

- Ethical principles require someone to exercise judgement  
- Governance structures depend on clear responsibility  
- Applied practice collapses when agency is ambiguous  
- Reflection and learning fail when ownership is unclear  

This book builds directly on **AI Capability: An Overview** and provides the conceptual foundation for later domain-specific books, particularly:

- **Applied Generative AI Practice**
- **Ethics, Equity, and Impact**
- **Decision-Making and Governance**

It can be read independently, but is most powerful when situated within the wider framework.

## Scope and Approach

This book focuses on **practice-facing agency**, not abstract theory.

It deliberately emphasises:

- Shared responsibility and decision boundaries  
- Human judgement, interpretation, and accountability  
- The socio-organisational conditions that shape AI use  
- How agency develops, stabilises, and erodes over time  

It deliberately avoids:

- Tool-specific guidance  
- Anthropomorphic “AI as partner” rhetoric  
- Claims about AI autonomy or replacement  
- Narrow technical framings of “human-in-the-loop”  

Co-agency is treated as a **capability to be designed and sustained**, not a safeguard that emerges automatically.

---

## Structure (Indicative)

The book is organised around:

- Defining human–AI co-agency clearly and practically  
- Examining how co-agency operates in real work  
- Identifying predictable risks and failure modes  
- Exploring how co-agency develops over time  
- Integrating co-agency with ethics, governance, and practice  
- Applying co-agency across education, research, and organisations  
- Supporting reflection and renewal  

The emphasis throughout is on **lived decision-making**, not abstract compliance.
