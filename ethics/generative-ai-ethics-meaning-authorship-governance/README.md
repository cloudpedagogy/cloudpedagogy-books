# Generative AI Ethics  
**Meaning, Authorship, and Governance**

An open-access book by **Jonathan Wong**, published by **CloudPedagogy**.

---

## Overview

*Generative AI Ethics: Meaning, Authorship, and Governance* examines the ethical, cultural, and governance challenges introduced by generative artificial intelligence systems, including large language models, image generators, and other creative AI tools.

The book focuses on how generative AI reshapes meaning-making, authorship, responsibility, power, and institutional practice across education, research, public service, and cultural production. Rather than treating ethics as a constraint applied after the fact, it frames ethical reflection as a core dimension of design, governance, and human–AI collaboration.

This book is written for educators, researchers, policymakers, designers, and institutions seeking to engage with generative AI in ways that are reflective, responsible, and future-oriented.

It forms part of the wider **CloudPedagogy** ecosystem of open, governance-aware resources supporting AI capability and ethical decision-making.

---

## Scope and Themes

The book explores:

- Ethical foundations relevant to generative AI  
- Meaning, authorship, and responsibility in AI-assisted creation  
- Data, consent, provenance, and cultural extraction  
- Bias, representation, and algorithmic justice  
- Accountability across the generative AI pipeline  
- Law, regulation, and policy futures  
- Institutional ethics in academia, industry, and the public sector  
- Emotional, psychological, and relational impacts of AI systems  
- Power, equity, and global perspectives  
- Ecological, posthuman, and regenerative ethics of technology  

---

## Structure

The book is organised into four main parts:

- **Foundations** – ethical traditions, responsible innovation, and what makes generative AI distinct  
- **Responsibility and Governance** – accountability, law, policy, and institutional practice  
- **Power, Equity, and Society** – inequality, representation, deepfakes, and emotional ethics  
- **Futures and Reflections** – education, aesthetics, posthuman perspectives, and regenerative ethics  

An epilogue reflects on ethical maturity and hopeful futures for human–AI collaboration.
